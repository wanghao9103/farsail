# Android unplugged offline investigation

2026-11-12. The user reports that an unplugged phone eventually disappears from the device list, not merely that a viewer reconnects. The connected Android 10 phone still had the same foreground connection and capture services after approximately 21 hours, battery exemption enabled, accepted heartbeats and no current network restriction. USB was supplying power during inspection, so this is not evidence of uninterrupted unplugged operation.

A 50-second forced deep-Doze sample retained accepted heartbeats about every 15 seconds; sampled heartbeat ages were 13878, 9085 and 4272 ms. The test restored `mForceIdle=false`, deep/light states ACTIVE. Physical charging remained connected. This did not reproduce the reported failure and does not exclude ColorOS policies when physically unplugged.

A separate confirmed defect was found: `SharingPower` acquired CPU and screen locks once for eight hours and never renewed them. The inspected long-running service no longer held either sharing lock. Replace them with non-reference-counted 90-second leases renewed every 30 seconds only while capture runs. Disabling screen retention, sleeping or entering keyguard releases the screen lease; renewal never wakes or unlocks the device. Stopping capture removes renewal callbacks and releases both locks. The connection service already renews its own bounded CPU lease and is not changed to hold an indefinite lock.

Add a bounded local connection journal for power/idle transitions, service creation/destruction, the last checkpointed heartbeat, heartbeat failures/recovery and long tick/heartbeat gaps. Store only times and coarse power/network flags; exclude accounts, tokens, endpoint addresses and Wi-Fi identifiers. Retain at most 24 events across process recreation and checkpoint successful heartbeats at most once per minute. Dump output includes capture power-lease state. The journal diagnoses suspension, process replacement and network/authentication failures after reconnecting USB; it does not bypass manufacturer restrictions.

Local validation passed 16 JVM tests, debug lint and instrumentation-test Kotlin compilation. Three new virtual-clock regression tests exercise 12 hours of capture, release on sleep/opt-out/stop, and bounded expiry when renewal stops. These are not a physical 12-hour battery test. Prepare an original-certificate `0.1.17-power1` candidate, retaining the previously accepted background wake fix. Physically unplugged device-list acceptance remains pending; no claim that the reported offline issue is solved.

## Observed interruption and firmware check

The signed `0.1.17-power1` candidate from `b9a9cadedba3cf4126ae41e997b622a5a12b9488` passed run `43221552837`; artifact `13945129328`, APK SHA-256 `c74a9f2c9bae7c7d3b518d3d239c8c99231927ba97670ab781c6e706effce697`. The original certificate and package metadata were verified before an in-place installation on the connected phone, retaining its UID and first-install time.

The new journal captured a 125177 ms tick gap and a 125174 ms heartbeat wall-clock gap after physical USB disconnection. Accepted heartbeats stopped at 21:02:04.839 and resumed at 21:04:10.015 after USB reconnection; the same services and process survived. Battery history recorded both capture and connection CPU locks ending approximately 300 seconds after screen-off, followed by CPU suspension. The coordinator source's device lease is 60 seconds, shorter than the observed interruption. There was no recorded heartbeat request failure during the gap.

Read-only inspection of the phone's actual ColorOS app-power page confirmed all four switches enabled: foreground wake, unrestricted background behavior, autostart and associated start. Android battery exemption and background/wake-lock app operations were also allowed. No permission or optimization switch was changed.

A second 100-second forced-Doze test with simulated battery disconnection retained heartbeats. A subsequent six-minute natural-idle test, with the user physically putting the phone to sleep and USB retained for diagnostics, reproduced loss of both CPU locks from the system power dump just after five minutes. App-local `isHeld` diagnostics still reported true. During this USB-connected simulation heartbeats continued, so it is not evidence of long-duration physical unplugged acceptance. Both tests restored battery reporting; final state was USB powered, no forced idle, and deep/light idle ACTIVE.

Read-only inspection of this phone's `coloros-services.jar` (SHA-256 `74967f39004e192ae8410a6b2f7f8cc860d5e22db78aaef548fd95371d65bb9d`) identified `ColorPartialWakeLockCheck`: screen-off monitoring skips charging, has a 300-second default threshold, and includes `whitelistWakelockRelease` and `confirmWakelockRelease` paths. These can release locks through `releaseWakeLockByGuardElf` and place the package/UID in a forbidden-held-lock list. This firmware behavior is consistent with the observed charging dependence and lock loss; the exact runtime branch was not traced. System files were not modified, and no fake audio, sensor activity, shell whitelist or hidden-permission workaround was added. Renewable capture locks fix the independent eight-hour expiry but do not establish a solution to this firmware restriction. A background wake design that the platform supports remains required for reliable unplugged availability.
